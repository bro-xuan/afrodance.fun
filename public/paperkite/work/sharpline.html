Sharpline Barbers

The brief

A busy three-chair barbershop near the station was losing customers to the phone: calls during cuts went unanswered, walk-ins didn't know how long they'd wait, and no-shows cost hours every week. They needed a site that books customers without anyone picking up the phone, and looks as sharp as the cuts.

The idea

Put booking front and centre, and make everything else answer what customers actually ask: what does it cost, how long is the wait, who's cutting, when are you open and how do I get there? Then give it the attitude of a gig poster, so it gets shared as much as it gets used.

01

Booked in 30 seconds

Pick a barber (or “anyone”), a service, a free slot and leave a phone number. Every “Book” button, from the price list to each barber's card, drops you into the flow with your choice already filled in, and a sticky button keeps booking one tap away on mobile.

02

Fewer no-shows

The confirmation gives a booking code, a one-tap calendar invite and a clear reminder-and-cancellation policy, so customers remember their slot and move it instead of just not turning up.

03

Walk-ins without the guesswork

A live queue at the top of the page shows free chairs or the current wait, so walk-ins know whether to come now or book. Staff stop answering “how long's the wait?” on the phone.

04

Easy to find, on Google too

Hours, address, a custom street map and call, WhatsApp and Instagram links sit in one place, and structured data gives Google the hours, location and full price list.

05

Built to be remembered

Giant type that warps as you move the mouse, a headline that bulks up as you scroll, a price list that draws each cut and glitchy barber portraits, all switched off for visitors who prefer reduced motion.
